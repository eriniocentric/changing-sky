What changed
How to read this Press ▶ to step through Rubin's exposures in time order. Each dot is one detection: something that was brighter or fainter than the reference image at that moment. Bright, large dots are in the current exposure; small dots were seen earlier.

Things that move leave a line: green for asteroids with known orbits, orange for candidate unknown movers found here by linking detections a few arcseconds apart at a steady speed across exposures 15–90 min apart. Things that stay put but keep changing (variable stars, active galaxies, supernovae) light up again at the same spot on later exposures and nights. Click any dot for its details and full history.
What this is notCandidate movers are unverified. A 3- or 4-point track at constant speed is very unlikely to be chance (none appeared in shifted-position controls), but it is not a discovery until the object is found again on other nights and given an orbit. Two-point pairs are weaker: on 30 Oct about 5% of such pairs are expected by chance. Known-asteroid positions come from Fink's monthly asteroid table, which so far ends on 31 Oct, so known asteroids are not drawn for November. Fink's cone search leaves out alerts already matched to known asteroids, so November candidates also exclude known objects. Dipoles and saturated detections (subtraction artefacts) are hidden.
