🛰 Satellites
What the satellite layer showsOrange lines are the paths of catalogued satellites that crossed one of Rubin's fields while lit by the Sun, during the ~30-second exposure, predicted from public orbital elements (Space-Track.org) seen from Cerro Pachón. Click a label to see which satellite it was. Satellites in Earth's shadow reflect no sunlight and are not drawn. In July (southern winter) almost all low satellites are in shadow during the night, so most tracks here belong to distant geostationary and high-orbit objects. Rubin itself suppresses alerts from known satellites; the Rubin-flagged glint trails alert type shows flashes its pipeline flagged anyway.
Find
How these alerts were chosenCollected from the Fink broker public API for the night of 2026-07-14 (UTC): six Fink science streams paged through the night, the latest objects in ~190 catalogue classes, and 378807 objects from 1-degree cone searches over the night's observed fields. Of 383,635 unique detections that night, artefacts were removed (subtraction dipoles, glint trails, saturated cores, SNR < 5), leaving 283,613; unidentified detections also had to have reliability ≥ 0.8 (194,279 left). One detection per object is shown (the strongest that night): 196,488 objects. Types combine Fink's cross-matches (TNS, VSX, blazar catalogues, HyperLEDA) with our own 1-arcsecond cross-match of every object against SIMBAD, Gaia DR3 (variability flag, star/quasar/galaxy probabilities, parallax) and the Milliquas quasar catalogue via the CDS XMatch service, because Fink's SIMBAD match was unavailable for this night. Asteroid alerts are not included: these query routes do not return Solar System detections. Fink recorded 403,821 objects that night, so this is a large but incomplete sample.
What am I looking at?Each alert compares a new image with a template built from earlier images of the same spot. The difference keeps only what changed (red: brighter now; blue: fainter). Each cutout is 6 arcseconds across. The light curve shows how the change evolves; colours mark Rubin's filters u g r i z y (ultraviolet → near-infrared). Fluxes are differences from the template, so they can be negative when a source has faded. The big sky view comes from other surveys and shows the surroundings.
